Beta & Gamma Functions & Improper Integrals

Eulerian integrals, relation between Beta and Gamma functions, duplication formula, 7 reduction integrals, and convergence of improper integrals.

1. Gamma Function & Recurrence Relations

The Gamma Function $\Gamma(n)$ (Euler’s integral of the second kind) generalizes factorials to positive real numbers.

  • Definition: $\Gamma(n) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x} x^{n-1}\,dx$ for $n > 0$.
  • Recurrence Relation: $\Gamma(n+1) = n\Gamma(n)$. For positive integers: $\Gamma(n+1) = n!$.
  • Special Value: $\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\pi}$.
  • Transformation: $\int_0^\infty e^{-k x} x^{n-1}\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(n)}{k^n}$.
Key Formula / Derivation Condition 
Gamma(n) = int_0^inf e^(-x) * x^(n-1) dx (n > 0)
Gamma(n+1) = n * Gamma(n) = n!
Gamma(1/2) = sqrt(pi)
int_0^inf e^(-kx) * x^(n-1) dx = Gamma(n) / k^n

2. Beta Function & Symmetry Property

The Beta Function $B(m,n)$ (Euler’s integral of the first kind) is defined for $m>0, n>0$.

  • Standard Algebraic Form: $B(m,n) = \int_0^1 x^{m-1} (1-x)^{n-1}\,dx$.
  • Symmetric Property: $B(m,n) = B(n,m)$.
  • Trigonometric Form: $B(m,n) = 2 \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^{2m-1}\theta \cos^{2n-1}\theta\,d\theta$.
  • Infinite Interval Form: $B(m,n) = \int_0^\infty \frac{y^{m-1}}{(1+y)^{m+n}}\,dy$.
Key Formula / Derivation Condition 
B(m,n) = int_0^1 x^(m-1) * (1-x)^(n-1) dx
B(m,n) = 2 * int_0^(pi/2) sin^(2m-1)(theta) * cos^(2n-1)(theta) dtheta
B(m,n) = B(n,m)

3. Beta-Gamma Core Relation & Duplication Formula

The fundamental bridge between Beta and Gamma functions enables fast evaluation of definite integrals.

  • Fundamental Relation: $B(m,n) = \frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}$.
  • Trigonometric Integral Formula: $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^p\theta \cos^q\theta\,d\theta = \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{p+q+2}{2}\right)}$.
  • Legendre’s Duplication Formula: $\Gamma(m)\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2m-1}} \Gamma(2m)$.
  • Euler’s Reflection Formula: $\Gamma(p)\Gamma(1-p) = \frac{\pi}{\sin(p\pi)}$ ($0 < p < 1$).
Key Formula / Derivation Condition 
B(m,n) = [Gamma(m) * Gamma(n)] / Gamma(m+n)
int_0^(pi/2) sin^p(t) * cos^q(t) dt = [Gamma((p+1)/2) * Gamma((q+1)/2)] / [2 * Gamma((p+q+2)/2)]
Gamma(m) * Gamma(m + 1/2) = (sqrt(pi) / 2^(2m-1)) * Gamma(2m)

4. Convergence of Improper Integrals

Improper integrals have unbounded intervals of integration or discontinuous integrands.

  • Comparison Test: If $0 \le f(x) \le g(x)$, then $\int_a^\infty g(x)\,dx$ converges $\implies \int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx$ converges.
  • $\mu$-Test: If $\lim_{x\to\infty} x^\mu f(x) = L$ (finite), then $\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx$ converges if $\mu > 1$ and diverges if $\mu \le 1$ ($L \ne 0$).
Key Formula / Derivation Condition 
int_a^inf (dx / x^p) converges for p > 1, diverges for p <= 1
mu-Test: lim_{x -> inf} x^mu * f(x) = L (converges if mu > 1 and L is finite)

2 & 5 Marks Exam Question 1

U3-Q1 & Q2: Definitions & Basic Beta-Gamma Evaluations
Verified Model Solution & Derivation:
Problem Statement: Define Beta function, Gamma function, 1st & 2nd kind improper integrals with examples, and evaluate $\Gamma(5)$, $\Gamma(7/2)$, and $B(5, 7)$.
PTU Evaluator Tip: Remember: $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$ for positive integers, and for half-integers peel off $(n-1)$ down to $\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$.
1. Definitions of Beta, Gamma & Improper Integrals
• Beta Function: $B(m, n) = \int_{0}^{1} x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}\,dx \quad (m>0, n>0)$.
• Gamma Function: $\Gamma(n) = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x} x^{n-1}\,dx \quad (n>0)$.
• 1st Kind Improper Integral: Infinite limit(s), e.g., $\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x^2} = 1$.
• 2nd Kind Improper Integral: Unbounded integrand on $[a,b]$, e.g., $\int_{0}^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}} = 2$.
2. Evaluate Γ(5) and Γ(7/2)
• $\Gamma(5) = 4! = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = \mathbf{24}$.
• $\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2}\right) = \frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{\pi}}$.
3. Evaluate B(5, 7)
$$B(5, 7) = \frac{\Gamma(5)\Gamma(7)}{\Gamma(5+7)} = \frac{4! \cdot 6!}{11!} = \frac{24 \times 720}{11 \times 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 \times 720} = \frac{24}{55440} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2310}}$$
5 Marks Exam Question 2

U3-Q3: All 7 Beta-Gamma Reduction Integrals
Verified Model Solution & Derivation:
Problem Statement: Evaluate:
(1) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^3 x \cos^5 x\,dx$; (2) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^5 x\,dx$; (3) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \cos^4 x\,dx$;
(4) $\int_{0}^{1} x^4(1-x)^3\,dx$; (5) $\int_{0}^{1} \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^5}}\,dx$; (6) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sqrt{\tan x}\,dx$; (7) $\int_{0}^{1} x^3(1-x)^5\,dx$.
PTU Evaluator Tip: Use the golden trig formula $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^p x \cos^q x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}{2\Gamma(\frac{p+q+2}{2})}$ for (1), (2), (3), and (6).
Integrals (1), (2), and (3) — Trigonometric Forms
• (1) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^3 x \cos^5 x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(2)\Gamma(3)}{2\Gamma(5)} = \frac{1! \cdot 2!}{2 \cdot 4!} = \frac{2}{48} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{24}}$.
• (2) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^5 x \cos^0 x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(3)\Gamma(1/2)}{2\Gamma(7/2)} = \frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{2 \cdot \frac{15}{8}\sqrt{\pi}} = \mathbf{\frac{8}{15}}$.
• (3) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^0 x \cos^4 x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(1/2)\Gamma(5/2)}{2\Gamma(3)} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} \cdot \frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\pi}}{2 \cdot 2!} = \mathbf{\frac{3\pi}{16}}$.
Integrals (4) and (7) — Direct Beta Function Forms
• (4) $\int_{0}^{1} x^{5-1}(1-x)^{4-1}\,dx = B(5,4) = \frac{4! \cdot 3!}{8!} = \frac{24 \times 6}{40320} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{280}}$.
• (7) $\int_{0}^{1} x^{4-1}(1-x)^{6-1}\,dx = B(4,6) = \frac{3! \cdot 5!}{9!} = \frac{6}{9 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{504}}$.
Integral (5) — Substitution x⁵ = t
Put $x^5 = t \implies x = t^{1/5}, \; dx = \frac{1}{5}t^{-4/5}\,dt$:
$$\int_{0}^{1} \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^5}}\,dx = \frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{1} t^{2/5}(1-t)^{-1/2}t^{-4/5}\,dt = \frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{1} t^{-2/5}(1-t)^{-1/2}\,dt = \mathbf{\frac{1}{5}B\left(\frac{3}{5}, \frac{1}{2}\right)}$$
Integral (6) — √(tan x) via Euler Reflection Formula
$$\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{1/2}x \cos^{-1/2}x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(3/4)\Gamma(1/4)}{2\Gamma(1)} = \frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi/4)} = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}}$$
10 Marks Exam Question 3

U3-Q4: All Properties & Proofs of Beta-Gamma Relations
Verified Model Solution & Derivation:
Problem Statement: Prove:
(I) $B(m, n) = B(n, m)$;
(II) $B(m, n) = 2\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{2m-1}\theta \cos^{2n-1}\theta\,d\theta$ & $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^p\theta\cos^q\theta\,d\theta$;
(III) $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)\Gamma(n-1)$;
(IV) $B(m, n) = \frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}$.
PTU Evaluator Tip: These 4 proofs together form the single most frequently asked 10-mark theory block in Unit-3 of IKG-PTU papers.
(I) Symmetry B(m, n) = B(n, m)
In $B(m,n) = \int_{0}^{1} x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}\,dx$, apply $\int_{0}^{a} f(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{a} f(a-x)\,dx$:
$$B(m,n) = \int_{0}^{1} (1-x)^{m-1}[1-(1-x)]^{n-1}\,dx = \int_{0}^{1} x^{n-1}(1-x)^{m-1}\,dx = B(n,m)$$
(II) Trigonometric Form of Beta Function
Put $x = \sin^2\theta \implies dx = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta$ ($\theta: 0 \to \frac{\pi}{2}$):
$$B(m,n) = 2\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{2m-1}\theta\cos^{2n-1}\theta\,d\theta$$
Setting $2m-1=p, 2n-1=q$ gives $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^p\theta\cos^q\theta\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}B\left(\frac{p+1}{2}, \frac{q+1}{2}\right)$.
(III) Recurrence Relation Γ(n) = (n - 1)Γ(n - 1)
Integrate $\Gamma(n) = \int_{0}^{\infty} x^{n-1} e^{-x}\,dx$ by parts ($u = x^{n-1}, dv = e^{-x}dx$):
$$\Gamma(n) = \left[-x^{n-1}e^{-x}\right]_{0}^{\infty} + (n-1)\int_{0}^{\infty} x^{n-2}e^{-x}\,dx = 0 + (n-1)\Gamma(n-1)$$
(IV) Fundamental Relation B(m, n) = Γ(m)Γ(n) / Γ(m + n)
$$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-(x+y)} x^{m-1} y^{n-1}\,dx\,dy$$
Put $x = u^2, y = v^2$, then convert to polar coordinates ($u = r\cos\theta, v = r\sin\theta$):
$$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = \left(2\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-r^2}r^{2(m+n)-1}\,dr\right)\left(2\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \cos^{2m-1}\theta\sin^{2n-1}\theta\,d\theta\right) = \Gamma(m+n)B(m,n)$$
5 Marks Exam Question 4

U3-Q5: Convergence of All Improper Integrals
Verified Model Solution & Derivation:
Problem Statement: Test convergence of:
(1) $\int_{0}^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x - x^2}}$;
(2) $\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x}}{(1+x)^2}\,dx$;
(3) $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\tan x}{x}\,dx$ and $\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\tan^{-1}x}{x^2+1}\,dx$.
PTU Evaluator Tip: Split integrals that have discontinuities at BOTH endpoints (like $x=0$ and $x=1$ in problem 1) at the midpoint $x = 1/2$ and test each half separately.
(1) ∫₀¹ dx / √(x - x²) — Discontinuous at both x = 0 and x = 1
Split at $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $I = \int_{0}^{1/2} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1-x}} + \int_{1/2}^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1-x}} = I_1 + I_2$.
• Near $x=0$: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1-x}} \sim \frac{1}{x^{1/2}}$ ($p = \frac{1}{2} < 1 \implies I_1$ converges).
• Near $x=1$: $\sim \frac{1}{(1-x)^{1/2}}$ ($p = \frac{1}{2} < 1 \implies I_2$ converges).
Hence $I$ Converges (value $= B(1/2, 1/2) = \pi$).
(2) ∫₀^∞ √x / (1 + x)² dx — 1st Kind Improper Integral
Continuous at $x = 0$. As $x \to \infty$, $\frac{x^{1/2}}{(1+x)^2} \sim \frac{x^{1/2}}{x^2} = \frac{1}{x^{3/2}}$.
Since $p = \frac{3}{2} > 1$, the integral Converges.
(3) ∫₀^(π/2) (tan x / x) dx vs ∫₀^∞ [tan⁻¹ x / (x² + 1)] dx
• For $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\tan x}{x}\,dx$: Singular at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Near $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$, $\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-x)} \sim \frac{1}{\frac{\pi}{2}-x}$ ($p = 1 \implies$ Divergent).
• For $\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\tan^{-1}x}{x^2+1}\,dx = \left[\frac{(\tan^{-1}x)^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{\infty} = \mathbf{\frac{\pi^2}{8}}$ (Convergent).
